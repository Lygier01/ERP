Lesson context

ERP modules translate enterprise architecture into business capability.

Sales

Coordinates customer orders, pricing, delivery commitments, and revenue-related processing.

Production

Plans and controls the transformation of materials, labor, and capacity into finished output.

Human Resources

Maintains workforce records, employment structures, attendance, payroll-related data, and accountability.

CRM

Manages relationship information, customer interactions, opportunities, and service continuity.

These modules build on shared data and business rules. Their value comes from coordinated flow, not from isolated screens.

First major concept

The Sales module manages the controlled movement from demand to customer commitment.

Definition

The Sales module is the ERP function that records, validates, prices, tracks, and monitors sales-related transactions from customer interest through order fulfillment and billing readiness.

Customer data

Account identity, contact details, credit rules, delivery preferences, and relationship status.

Commercial records

Quotations, sales orders, price lists, terms, taxes where configured, and fulfillment status.

Operational outputs

Order backlog, delivery commitments, sales performance, availability checks, and billing triggers.

Customer-facing integration

The CRM module manages customer relationships before, during, and after sales processing.

Definition

Customer Relationship Management in ERP organizes customer interactions, contact history, opportunity records, service information, and relationship status so that commercial decisions use consistent customer knowledge.

Relationship records

Contacts, organizations, preferences, communication history, ownership, and relationship status.

Opportunity control

Pipeline stages, probability, expected value, responsible owner, and next commercial action.

Service continuity

Issue tracking, support history, response commitments, and customer satisfaction indicators.

ERP connection

Qualified customer and demand information can become sales orders, forecasts, and reporting inputs.

Operations and manufacturing

The Production module controls how planned output is translated into organized work.

Definition

The Production module is the ERP function that plans material requirements, schedules work, authorizes production, records execution, and connects manufacturing operations with inventory and cost information.

Product structure

Bills of materials, routings, operations, work centers, and production versions define how output is made.

Planning logic

Demand, inventory, capacity, lead time, and procurement rules determine production requirements.

Execution records

Work orders, material issue, labor time, completion, quality status, and production variance.

People and organizational structure

The HR module maintains the workforce information that supports roles, capacity, payroll-related data, and accountability.

Definition

The Human Resources module is the ERP function that maintains employee master data, positions, organizational responsibilities, attendance-related records, payroll inputs, training data, and role-based workforce information.

Employee master

Identity, position, department, employment status, reporting line, and assigned work location.

Time and attendance

Schedules, worked time, absences, approvals, and time records used by payroll-related processes.

Payroll inputs

Compensation structure, allowances, deductions, benefits, and approved time data where configured.

Access and roles

Responsibility roles, system permissions, segregation of duties, and user accountability.

Integrated visual explanation

ERP module integration follows recurring data-flow patterns.

Demand signal

Relationship and customer relationship records can become formal demand data used by sales planning and production scheduling.

Availability signal

Production and inventory information influences sales commitments, delivery dates, and customer communication.

Workforce signal

Employee roles, time data, skills, and responsibility roles support production execution and customer-facing accountability.

Transaction signal

Sales orders, work orders, confirmations, and service updates generate records for reporting and management visibility.

Control signal

Approval rules, system roles, and audit trails determine who can create, change, release, or close records.

Performance signal

Module outputs become operational metrics for delivery reliability, capacity, relationship quality, and workforce utilization.

Challenges and limitations

Module integration requires disciplined data, process, and responsibility management.

Data ownership

Customer, item, and employee records must have clear owners to prevent duplicates and conflicting definitions.

Process handoffs

Sales, production, HR, and CRM must agree on status meanings, approval points, timing, and exception handling.

Capacity accuracy

Production plans become unreliable when material, labor, routing, or work-center data is incomplete.

Role security

Access must protect employee records, customer information, pricing rules, and commercially sensitive records.

User discipline

ERP outputs depend on timely encoding, proper transaction closure, and consistent use of official records.

Change control

Changes in products, roles, prices, production rules, and workflows must be documented and governed.

Lesson summary

Session 5 key concepts

Module purposes

Sales controls customer commitments, Production manages planned output, HR maintains workforce data, and CRM organizes relationship knowledge.

Shared data foundation

Customer, item, employee, organizational, and transaction records must remain consistent across modules to support reliable operations.

Integration value

These modules connect demand, fulfillment, work execution, people, controls, and management reporting into one coordinated ERP system.

ERP modules are not independent islands; they are specialized views of one integrated organizational system.
