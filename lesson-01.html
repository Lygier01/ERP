Inventory

Finance

Duplicate work

Conflicting data

Sales says 2M revenue; Finance says 1.8M. Who's right?

3

Manual consolidation means days of delay

4

Poor decisions

Outdated or inconsistent data leads to wrong actions

5

No traceability

Errors are hard to trace across disconnected files

6

Scaling fails

More branches means more chaos with separate systems

Definition

Enterprise Resource Planning

Formal

An integrated information system that connects the major business functions of an organization through a shared database, enabling real-time data flow across departments and supporting coordinated planning, execution, and control.

Enterprise

The whole organization, not one team

Resource

Money, people, materials, time, information

Planning

Coordinating, deciding, forecasting, controlling

7 / 24
Key characteristics

What makes a system qualify as ERP?

1

Integration

Modules are connected. A transaction in one area automatically affects related areas.

2

Shared database

All modules read from and write to the same source. No parallel files.

3

Real-time processing

Updates are immediate. No waiting for batch runs or manual entry.

4

Cross-functional

Covers multiple areas: finance, sales, inventory, HR, procurement, production.

Misconceptions

What ERP is NOT

Not just accounting software

Accounting is one module. ERP covers sales, inventory, HR, procurement, production, and more.

Not just for big companies

Cloud and open-source ERP (Odoo, ERPNext) are accessible to SMEs and schools.

Not a magic fix

Requires good processes, clean data, and trained users. Technology alone is not enough.

Not one single brand

ERP is a concept. SAP, Oracle, Odoo are different implementations of the same idea.

Reality check

ERP only succeeds with the right conditions

  • Clean data (garbage in = garbage out)
  • Trained users who follow procedures
  • Management commitment to using ERP outputs
  • Well-defined business processes before implementation
  • Ongoing maintenance and continuous improvement

Technology is the tool. People and processes determine whether it succeeds or fails.

1960s
Stage 1

MRP: Material Requirements Planning

Purpose

Calculate what materials are needed, how many, and when, based on production schedules and bills of materials.

Input

Production schedule, Bill of Materials, inventory levels

Output

Planned purchase orders, production orders, reschedules

Benefit

Reduced stockouts and excess inventory

Limitation: assumed unlimited factory capacity. No labor, machine, or financial planning.

1980s
Stage 2

MRP II: Manufacturing Resource Planning

What changed

Extended MRP to include machine capacity, labor scheduling, shop floor control, and basic costing. Still manufacturing-only.

DimensionMRPMRP II
ScopeMaterials onlyMaterials + machines + labor + cost
CapacityAssumes unlimitedChecks against actual capacity
Financial linkNoneBasic manufacturing cost
Key question"What materials do we need?""Can we produce this, and at what cost?"

Limitation: still factory-only. Sales, full finance, HR, and distribution were not covered.

26 / 48
1990s
Stage 3

ERP: Enterprise Resource Planning

The big leap

Cross-functional integration of ALL major business functions into one platform with a shared database. No longer manufacturing-only.

Added: Full Finance

GL, AP, AR, budgeting, reporting

Added: Sales

Orders, pricing, delivery, invoicing

Added: HR

Employees, payroll, recruitment

Limitation: internally focused. No connection to customers, suppliers, or partners outside the organization. Expensive and rigid.

27 / 48
Why the 1990s?

Technology and business conditions converged

Technology ready

  • Client-server computing matured
  • Relational databases stable
  • LANs connected departments
  • GUI made systems usable

Business demanded it

  • Globalization increased complexity
  • Competition demanded speed
  • Y2K forced system upgrades
  • Process reengineering trend

2010s+
Stage 5

Cloud ERP / SaaS ERP

Definition

ERP delivered as a service. Provider manages software, infrastructure, and maintenance. Organization subscribes and accesses via browser or app.

Advantages

  • Lower upfront cost (subscription)
  • Scalable on demand
  • Anywhere access
  • Faster deployment

Concerns

  • Internet dependency
  • Data stored with provider
  • Ongoing subscription cost
  • Customization limits

Key takeaway

Why this history matters for you

Understand design

Modern ERP features exist because of specific historical problems. The "why" helps you evaluate systems.

Predict trends

The pattern is clear: systems expand as complexity grows. Next: AI-driven ERP, IoT, industry-specific platforms.

Systems evolve because organizations evolve.

If you work in ERP, always start by understanding the business need, not the technology.
