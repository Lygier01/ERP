Finance module

Finance provides the organization’s unified financial record.

Definition

The Finance module records, classifies, controls, summarizes, and reports the monetary effects of enterprise operations.

General Ledger (GL)

The central accounting record containing accounts, journal entries, balances, periods, and financial statements.

Accounts Payable (AP)

Manages supplier obligations, invoices, due dates, payment status, and related control records.

Accounts Receivable (AR)

Manages customer claims, invoices, collections, aging, credit status, and outstanding balances.

Finance does not operate only at period end. Integrated modules create financial entries as authorized operational events occur.

Procurement module

Procurement governs how organizational needs become authorized purchases.

Definition

The Procurement module manages supplier information, purchasing requests, approvals, purchase orders, receipts, invoice matching, and purchasing performance data.

Supplier management

Maintains approved supplier records, commercial terms, contacts, status, and performance information.

Purchase authorization

Routes requested goods and services through policy-based review, budget, and approval controls.

Purchase order control

Creates the formal purchasing document that defines item, quantity, price, delivery, and responsibility.

Receipt coordination

Connects delivered quantities and accepted services to the original purchase order.

Invoice matching

Compares purchasing, receipt, and supplier invoice records before financial recognition and payment.

Purchasing visibility

Provides status, commitments, lead times, exceptions, and supplier performance information.

Inventory module

Inventory maintains trusted records of stock quantity, location, status, and value.

Definition

The Inventory module records and controls items as they are received, stored, transferred, reserved, issued, counted, adjusted, and valued.

Item master

Defines item identity, description, units, classifications, controls, and relevant planning settings.

Location structure

Represents warehouses, storage areas, bins, branches, and other controlled stock locations.

Quantity status

Distinguishes on-hand, available, reserved, in-transit, blocked, or inspection quantities.

Stock movement

Records receipts, transfers, issues, returns, and adjustments with dates, references, and users.

Valuation

Connects physical quantities to costing rules and financial inventory balances.

Replenishment

Uses demand, minimum levels, lead times, and planning rules to signal supply needs.

Inventory control structure

Reliable stock information requires aligned records, movements, and verification.

Identity
Item code, description, unit of measure, classification, and control attributes
Prevents duplicate or ambiguous item records
Location
Warehouse, storage area, bin, branch, ownership, and stock status
Shows where stock is and whether it may be used
Movement
Receipt, transfer, issue, reservation, return, adjustment, and reference document
Explains every increase, decrease, and change of custody
Verification
Cycle counts, physical counts, reconciliation, approvals, and exception analysis
Keeps system records aligned with controlled physical evidence

Challenges and considerations

Module integration requires disciplined data, timing, authorization, and reconciliation.

Duplicate master records

Multiple supplier or item identities fragment history and create inconsistent balances.

Timing differences

Late receipts, invoices, postings, or adjustments can make operational and financial views temporarily disagree.

Authorization gaps

Weak role design may allow incompatible actions or bypass purchasing and payment controls.

Valuation errors

Incorrect units, costs, accounts, or movement types can distort stock and financial results.

Unresolved exceptions

Quantity, price, receipt, and invoice mismatches require ownership and timely resolution.

Configuration drift

Uncontrolled changes to posting, workflow, and master data rules weaken consistency and auditability.
